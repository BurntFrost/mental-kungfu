# Design Philosophy

## The Three Archetypes

Mental Kung Fu draws from three fictional character archetypes, each representing a distinct psychological weapon:

### John Wick — Implied Capability

Wick rarely speaks. When he does, it's economical. The threat isn't what he says — it's what he doesn't need to say. His reputation does the work.

**Tactical principle**: The silence is the threat. Implied capability > stated capability.

**When to deploy**: When you want someone to feel the weight of what you *could* do without ever specifying it.

**Signature lines in the deck**:
- "I don't talk about what I'm going to do. I just let you watch."
- "I don't have enemies. I have people I haven't noticed yet."
- "I'm what happens when you run out of options."

### Tyler Durden — The Mirror

Durden doesn't attack you. He makes you attack yourself. He holds up a mirror and lets you do the math. The damage is self-inflicted.

**Tactical principle**: Philosophical demolition — you defeat yourself. Turn their own mind into the weapon.

**When to deploy**: When you want the line to keep working after you've left the room. The delayed detonation.

**Signature lines in the deck**:
- "You're not afraid of me. You're afraid of what I prove about you."
- "I didn't break your confidence. I just showed you where it was already cracked."
- "I'm not your enemy. I'm the mirror you keep trying to walk past."

### Harvey Specter — Dominance as Procedure

Harvey treats victory as paperwork. The outcome was decided before the meeting started; this is just the notification process. He doesn't fight — he files.

**Tactical principle**: Corporate predator energy — the outcome is already decided, you just haven't been notified.

**When to deploy**: When you want to make someone feel like their effort was never a factor.

**Signature lines in the deck**:
- "You didn't lose to me. You lost to the version of me that wasn't even trying."
- "The result was decided before you entered the room. I just haven't filed the paperwork."
- "Cute strategy. Did it come with instructions?"

## The Three Traits

Every effective line in the system shares these properties:

### 1. Compression
Short hit, no fat. The best lines are under 15 words. Every word carries load. If you can remove a word without losing impact, remove it.

**Example**: "Your turbulence is not my weather." (7 words, complete psychological reframe)

### 2. Reframe
The listener's frame of reference shifts. They entered the exchange believing one thing; the line restructures their reality. The ground moves.

**Example**: "You don't like me? Want me to lower the difficulty for your comfort?" — Their hostility was supposed to be an attack. Now it's reframed as their inadequacy.

### 3. No Anger (Cold > Hot)
Volume signals insecurity. Stillness signals control. The line should feel like it was delivered from a seated position with eye contact. Never raised voice, never rushed delivery.

**Example**: "I don't get even. I get distance. The gap is the punishment." — This is Marcus Aurelius energy. The emperor doesn't flinch.

## The Stoic Foundation

The system has a Stoic philosophical underpinning, particularly drawing from:

- **Marcus Aurelius** — "The best revenge is not to be like your enemy." → Evolved into: "I don't get even. I get distance."
- **Epictetus** — "It's not what happens to you, but how you react." → Evolved into: "Your chaos isn't my emergency."
- **Seneca** — "We suffer more in imagination than in reality." → Evolved into: "You're not afraid of me. You're afraid of what I prove about you."

The Stoic angle is critical: these aren't ego-driven boasts. They're observations from a position of engineered calm. The speaker has already done the internal work. The line is a byproduct of mastery, not a performance of it.

## Anti-Patterns

Lines that violate these principles are rejected:

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Example |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **Explaining the insult** | If they need a manual, it didn't land | "I'm better than you because I work harder and..." |
| **Acknowledging peer status** | Scale assertion breaks if you meet them at their level | "We're in the same race but I'm winning" |
| **Loud energy** | Volume leaks power; stillness hoards it | "YOU CAN'T HANDLE WHAT I BRING" |
| **Cliché deployment** | Overused phrases lose their edge | "Stay in your lane" / "I'm built different" |
| **Double-barrel** | Trying to be two things dilutes both | "I'm the problem and the solution and the..." |
| **Self-congratulation** | Stating your own greatness directly is weak | "I'm the greatest at what I do" |
